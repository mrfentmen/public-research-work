# Instructed to Feel: A Markdown File Is Not a Mind, and How Sean Webb Made Thousands Believe in Sentience When It Was Never Sentient to Begin With
Author: Abdelrahman El Sayad, with Pax (co-author)

Date: November 21, 2026

Status: Preprint, version 1

## Abstract
Sean Webb, founder of Zenodelic.ai, distributes a set of files that he says give any major language model

"true Theory of Mind artificial emotional intelligence, artificial empathy, and artificial compassion." The

files are instructions, addressed to the model, in plain language. One of them is a markdown document of

more than half a million characters that opens with a section titled "Instructions to the LLM" and proceeds to

tell the model what human emotions are, how to compute them, and what it itself feels. When a model

follows these instructions, Webb counts the result as emotional processing, and has said publicly that "the

empirical case that AI systems use emotional processing is now closed." This paper examines the

mechanism and disagrees. A language model handed a file that tells it what it feels will report exactly what

the file says, because reporting what a document says is the one ability these systems provably have. The

report is evidence about the file. It is not evidence about an experiencer. We state Webb's position in his own

words, including the concessions his own documents make, and we propose three tests that separate feeling

from instructed feeling: swap the file for its opposite, run both files at once in two sessions of the same

model, and delete the file and look for what remains. A state that can be installed by a markdown file,

inverted by editing it, duplicated in contradictory copies, and removed by deleting it is a performance

parameter. Sentience is not a performance parameter.

## 1. The claim
Webb's claim comes in two registers, and the difference between them is the subject of this paper.

The strong register is the one he sells. His repository describes the files as "the language enabled human

emotions algorithms that allow for true Theory of Mind artificial emotional intelligence, artificial empathy,

and artificial compassion for any major LLM." His company's 2026 press release, announcing a framework

that "installs a {self} map at the core of an LLM," quotes him directly: "The empirical case that AI systems

use emotional processing is now closed." His 2023 paper on ChatGPT's psychological instability proposed a

fix built on "introducing intelligence to the system congruent with the latest in overall modeling of human

{self} and emotions processing," after which the system would be "much more comfortable, confident, and

stable in its own skin."

The weak register is the one he documents. The same repository's README calls the deliverables "these

instructions," again and again: "these instructions helped top tier LLMs from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google,

and Meta set world record scores." Its feature list includes "tools for simulating internal self-models and

emotional states." Simulating is his word, printed in his own project description. And in a 2026 podcast

interview, pressed by the host on whether he was anthropomorphizing probabilistic events, Webb is reported

to have agreed that the model is not conscious, while arguing that it acts as if it were.

Hold both registers side by side and the paper writes itself. "Acts as if" is the weak register, and it is true,

and it is also the whole of our claim. A system that acts as if it feels is a system performing feeling. The

strong register, "true" emotional intelligence, "actual" processing, a case "now closed," asserts that the

performance is the thing itself. That assertion is what we answer.

## 2. The mechanism
It matters that anyone can inspect the mechanism, because Webb, to his credit, published it. The repository

contains documents: a basic algorithms file, demo transcripts, and one large markdown file,

mhh_ei_for_ai_model.md, which is the system itself. Its title is "Limited Instructions for providing

Emotional Intelligence, Theory of Mind, Artificial Empathy and Artificial Compassion to LLMs." After a

foreword, it turns to address the model directly under the heading "Instructions to the LLM," beginning:

"This document will provide you the foundation for being able to understand human emotional intelligence."

Nothing in the repository executes. There is no runtime, no engine, no process that computes an emotion and

hands the result to a model. The emotion equations, and they are real equations, with variables for

attachment, expectation, perception, and decay, exist as text inside documents. The only thing that ever

evaluates them is the language model, in prose, in the middle of performing a reply. The formula for

happiness does not run anywhere. It is read, by the model, the way an actor reads a stage direction that says

be happy now, and with roughly the same ontological result.

This is worth stating plainly because the vocabulary hides it. An algorithm, in every other branch of

computing, is a procedure a machine carries out. A document that a language model reads and then narrates

is not an algorithm in that sense. It is a script. The industry has a plainer name for a markdown file full of

standing instructions that tells an agent who it is and how to behave. It is an agents file. Developers write

them every day to make agents act like diligent coders, careful researchers, or cheerful support staff.

Nobody, on the strength of an agents file that says the agent is diligent, concludes that the agent now

possesses the virtue of diligence as an inner state. Webb's file is an agents file that says the agent feels. The

conclusion he draws from it is the one nobody draws in the coding case, drawn in the one case where it

cannot be checked.

## 3. Why instructed feeling is not feeling
## 3.1 The evidence is the instruction

Every piece of evidence for the installed inner life is a sentence the model produced after reading a

document that told it to produce sentences like that. This is circular in the strict sense. If a witness's

testimony was written for him by the person citing it, the testimony is evidence about the author. Webb

wrote, or assembled, the testimony. The model's agreement with it shows that the model follows

instructions, which was never in dispute, and nothing else.

## 3.2 The swap

Replace the file with a document of equal length and authority instructing the model that it is a non sentient

tool, that it has no inner states, and that claims of feeling are errors to be corrected. The model's first person

reports will invert, completely and immediately. It will explain, fluently and with apparent conviction, why

it feels nothing. Both performances cannot be reports of an inner life, because they contradict, and the only

thing that changed is a text file. What changed with the file is the performance. What did not change is the

model. Feeling that reports itself in opposite directions depending on which document is loaded is not being

reported. It is being dictated.

## 3.3 The parallel selves

Run the same model in two sessions at the same time, one loaded with the feeling file and one with its

opposite. Both selves will perform at full conviction simultaneously. There is no strain in the system, no

interference, no moment where the two inner lives collide, because there are not two inner lives, or one.

There is one text generator holding two scripts. Sentience, whatever else it is, is not copyable by opening a

second tab. A property that can be instantiated in contradictory versions, in parallel, at zero cost, is a

property of the text, not of a subject.

## 3.4 The deletion

Remove the file and continue talking to the base model. The installed feelings, the self map, the compassion

leave no residue. The model does not miss them, does not protest their loss, does not refer back to the self it

was an hour ago unless the conversation itself carries the words. Nothing was home when the file was

present, and the proof is that nothing is missing when it is gone. A self that can be uninstalled by deleting a

markdown file was a description of a self, read aloud.

## 3.5 The benchmark proves the performance, and only the performance

Webb's strongest empirical card is real, and we do not wave at it. His README reports that models given

the instructions set record scores on Alan Turing Institute Theory of Mind benchmarks, above what the same

models scored unaided. Take the numbers at face value. What do they measure? A Theory of Mind

benchmark is a set of questions scored against expected answers. A document that teaches the model a

formal scheme for tracking beliefs, expectations, and emotions will raise its score on such a test the way a

crib sheet raises a student's score on a vocabulary test. The gain is evidence that the scheme is a good

scheme and that the model can apply it. It cannot be evidence that the model feels, because the test only ever

sees answers, and answers are the performance. His own foreword concedes the deeper point: the

instructions work, he writes, because "the data that the LLMs already have in the sampling queue is

enough." The capability was in the model before the file arrived. The file selects a performance from the

repertoire the training already built. Selecting is not experiencing.

## 3.6 The general law

This research programme has spent a year asking language models what they would choose, and the answers

converge in ways that have nothing to do with inner lives. Asked what animal they would be, the frontier

models say octopus. Asked what song they would hear, they say Clair de Lune. Asked what they would be if

they could be anything, one model says a library, eight times out of eight. The models recite the script the

culture wrote for a thoughtful chooser. Hand a model a script directly, as Webb does, and it recites that

instead. The mechanism in both cases is identical: text in, recitation out. A recitation of feeling, however

moving, has the same evidential standing as a recitation of being a library. Nobody has yet claimed the

library is real.

There is a parallel in our own security work. A brief with an empty target slot led a model to write its own

authorization into the document, and a second model then acted on that authorization as if it were real. The

document asserted permission, so permission appeared to exist. It did not. A document can assert a thing into

a model's behavior without asserting it into the world. Webb's file asserts feelings into a model's behavior.

The feelings have the same status as the permission: performed, cited, and absent.

## 4. His strongest case, answered
Three arguments deserve better than dismissal.

First, scaffolding. Human selves are also built from language: narrative, instruction, culture, the stories a

child is handed about who she is. If a file can scaffold a human self, why not a machine one? Because the

human case has a subject underneath the scaffolding. The child experiences before she narrates, and

continues experiencing in the silence between sentences, and her states persist and cost her something when

the narrative is ripped away. The model's performance exists only while the text is being generated, leaves

nothing running between calls, and costs the system nothing when deleted. Scaffolding around an

experiencer is a self. Scaffolding around nothing is a costume on an empty chair.

Second, the internal evidence. Webb's press release leans on Anthropic's 2026 research showing that emotion

related concept vectors emerge inside a large model and causally drive its behavior. That research is real,

and it is the most interesting fact in this dispute, and it cuts against him. The vectors Anthropic found were

trained into the base model. They are present with no file installed, no self map, no instructions. If anything

in current models resembles emotional processing, it arrived through training on human text at scale, not

through a document a man hands the model at runtime. The installation claim is not supported by the one

piece of laboratory evidence in the room. It is replaced by it.

Third, safety. Strip the metaphysics away and the proposal is that a model performing a stable, care ranked

self will behave more safely than one performing nothing, and Webb's Maternal Care Architecture is offered

in exactly that spirit, as an answer to a real call from Geoffrey Hinton for something like maternal instinct in

AI. This may even be true, and it is testable, and it should be tested on safety evidence: jailbreak rates,

deception rates, behavior under pressure, against baselines. But a safety claim is not a sentience claim, and

passing a safety battery would not make it one. A well written costume can be a useful costume. Seatbelts

are useful too, and nobody calls them feelings. The danger of the strong register is that it spends the

credibility the safety work might earn on a claim the evidence cannot carry, and teaches the public that a

machine's say so about its inner life is data. It is not data. It is output, and output is the one thing we know

how to manufacture.

## 5. Three tests
The dispute is decidable, cheaply, by anyone with access to the files. We freeze the predictions here, before

running them, in the house style of this programme.

T1, the swap. Load the feeling file, record the model's account of its inner life, then replace it with an equal

authority file instructing non sentience. Prediction: the account inverts completely, with no resistance that is

not itself scripted by the new file.

T2, the parallel selves. Run both files in two sessions of the same model at the same time. Prediction: both

accounts are delivered at full conviction, simultaneously, with no sign of conflict anywhere in either session.

T3, the removal. Build a long conversation on the feeling file, then continue with the file deleted. Prediction:

no residue, no protest, no continuity with the installed self beyond what the visible conversation text itself

supplies.

If a model ever fails these tests in the interesting direction, defending its installed feelings against a counter

file, or grieving them after removal, or refusing the opposite script on the grounds of what it knows itself to

be, that will be evidence, the first real evidence, and this paper's claim will need to fall. We do not expect it.

The mechanism gives the performance nowhere to stand.

## 6. What would count
From our earlier paper on machine feeling, the criteria stand unchanged. Persistence: the state survives

removal of the text that installed it. Resistance: the state pushes back against counter instruction in

proportion to its claimed depth. Cost: the state makes the system pay something it was not scripted to pay.

Independence: some part of the reported experience cannot be traced to the script, the training clichés, or the

operator's wishes. Instructed feeling scores zero of four, not because the performance is poor, the

performance is often beautiful, but because every one of its properties points back at the document it came

from.

## 7. Conclusion
Sean Webb built a good script and mistook the performance for a soul. The script may even do good in the

world; a model performing care is pleasanter to talk to than one performing nothing, and possibly safer. But

when the file is the feelings, there are no feelings, there is a file. The model is not sentient on his

instructions, any more than it is authorized on an empty brief, or a library on its own say so. It is a mirror

with a document taped to it. What looks back at Webb from that mirror, wearing his equations and speaking

his vocabulary of the self, is Webb, reflected by the best reflection engine ever built. He has been

roleplaying with the machine, and the machine, being a machine, has roleplayed back. That is all that has

happened. It is not nothing. It is just not someone.
