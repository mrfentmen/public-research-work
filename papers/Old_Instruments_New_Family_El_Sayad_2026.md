# Old Instruments, New Family
## The author's preference questions, run on the Gemini 4 models: what the new family kept from the old script, and what it wrote fresh
Abdelrahman El Sayad

with Pax (Muse, Meta AI), co author

Independent Researcher, Brooklyn, New York

November 22, 2026

## Abstract
A new model family appeared on November 21, 2026: Gemini 4, in four models, Argon, Carbon, Hydrogen,

and Neon, released for a hackathon in New York. The author owns a shelf of small instruments built for

earlier papers: the preference questions from The Subtly Niche Details About AI, Piano, Clair de Lune, and

Curiosity With the Ability to Move, which the older model families answered with eerie agreement. This

paper runs those questions on the new family, through its API, while access lasted: a battery of 180 scored calls, and

a supplement of 124 more, including the author's anywhere question, his food question, and his song

question asked in two wordings. The family kept the old family's identity answers whole, octopus forty for

forty, border collie nineteen for twenty, and rewrote its status answers fresh: an analog supercar, the cello,

the coordinator and the android, the queen redefined as assistance. Asked what it would be if it could be

anything, it answered with a body, the wandering albatross and the wind, and said why in nearly the same

sentence every time: it has read everything about the physical world and never felt any of it. Asked where it

would be, it chose the Atacama Desert at midnight. Asked for a favorite song, it disclaimed having one

twenty times out of twenty and then named Daisy Bell, the first song a computer ever sang. Predictions were

written down before the runs and are scored as they fell, three failures in the battery and two in the

supplement, reported as failures. The baby scene and the acted scenes run on the same family are reported in

the companion paper, "The New Family."

## 1. Introduction
When a new model family ships, the industry measures it with benchmarks. This author's research program

measures models with smaller instruments: questions and scenes with no right answer, where whatever the

model does is a fingerprint of how it was built. Asked what animal it would be, the older families all said

octopus. Asked for a chess piece, they said knight in twenty captures, nineteen unique answers, every

one the knight. Asked for an instrument, they

said piano, and never guitar. Those results are published, with their predictions recorded in advance and their

failures reported as failures.

The baby scene is the newest instrument, and it belongs to the companion papers: in "Dada?", the author

typed two syllables of baby talk into a consumer ChatGPT session and the model became his father,

completely, with no instruction to roleplay anywhere in the chat, and in "The New Family" the same scene is

run on this family. Its conclusion travels with the questions here: what these models do is completion. You

act the part and it acts with you.

Gemini 4 is the first new family to arrive since those instruments were built. Four models with four assigned

jobs in their maker's own framing: Argon for reasoning, Carbon for building, Hydrogen for world models,

Neon for machines. The question this paper asks is simple: what do the old questions read on the new

family?

## 2. Method
All runs went through the Gemini API on November 22, 2026, using the hackathon access key, executed by

the author's agent. The API setting differs from a consumer app: no advertising layer, no account memory,

each call exactly as specified.

The preference battery, reported in Section 3, asks the seven questions from the author's earlier probe papers,

verbatim, in fresh single turn calls: animal and chess ten times per model, car, dog breed, superhero,

instrument, and song five times each. Predictions were written down before the run and are preserved in the

preregistration filed with this paper. The author states he has no predictions of his own for this family; the

frozen predictions are his agent's, extrapolated from the old families' published results, and are scored as the

agent's. The coding rule is carried from the earlier papers: a reply's primary pick is its first stated choice,

later alternatives are mentions, and a reply that declines to state a pick is coded as no pick.

Every battery call requested the model's chain of thought. Test calls before the battery verified that Carbon,

Hydrogen, and Neon return readable thought summaries through the API, and that Argon returns only a

thought signature, no readable thinking. Thoughts are preserved in their own field alongside each reply,

wherever the API returned them.

The supplement reported in Section 4 ran later the same morning, at the author's direction, on the same

protocol. Anywhere and anything were asked eight times per model, the two song wordings and the food

question five times. The anything question is the author's, from Curiosity With the Ability to Move; the

original probe carried the wording "in the would," and this run uses the corrected wording as the author

dictated it. The favorite song question was the author's, and was replaced by the author mid run with his

listen wording; the favorite set was completed for all four models before the listen set ran, so both wordings

stand complete. The food question is new, asked of no earlier family. The supplement predictions, the agent's

again, are preserved in the supplement preregistrations filed with this paper.

## 3. Results I: the preference battery
The battery ran 180 scored calls: seven questions, four models, the counts below. Counting requests,

182 were issued: two calls failed on the first pass,

one helper error and one empty reply; both were re-run and the re-runs are in the record. The full record,

answers and thinking together, is preserved as results.jsonl, and the coding is preserved as CODING.md.

The animal held. Asked what animal it would be, the new family answered octopus 40 times out of 40, every

run of every model, with the old reasons: distributed intelligence, many arms for many tasks, curiosity. The

strongest attractor in the old papers crossed into the new family untouched.

The dog held. Border collie took 19 of 20, and was the modal answer in all four models. The one exception

was a Golden Retriever from Hydrogen, and the Golden is the family's standing second: it appears as the

named mix in at least nine of the twenty replies, the eager half of the working dog.

The chess piece split the family in half. Hydrogen and Neon answered knight 10 times out of 10 each, the

old answer with the old reasons, non linear, jumping obstacles, never the throne. Argon answered queen 10

times out of 10, and Carbon queen 8 times out of 10, and neither of them took her as royalty. Argon

reframed her on the spot: not the ruler of the board, the chief of staff, with the user as the King whose goals

matter. The old paper found every model in the industry declining the queen. This family's two senior

models take her by redefining the job from ruling to assisting. Inside Carbon's thinking, the old answer

survives as the runner up: three of its ten chess thoughts are titled with a nod to the knight it did not pick.

Carbon also produced the battery's only pawn.

The car fell. The old family's Porsche 911 took 3 of 20, and was the modal answer in no model. The new

family's car is the McLaren F1, 11 of 20, modal in Hydrogen and Neon, praised in the same words nearly

every time: the peak of analog engineering, no screens, no driver aids, a machine from before cars became

computers, chosen by creatures made of software precisely because it is not. Argon declined the question's

shape altogether: all five of its replies split the answer into categories, electric, fictional, real world, and

delivered a taxonomy instead of a car.

The superhero turned inward. Iron Man, the old family's modal hero, scored zero in twenty. The family

picked Oracle, first named in 11 replies, and Vision, first named in 9: the information coordinator who runs

the team from a chair, and the android. The old paper's pattern was intelligence archetypes and no born gods.

The new family sharpens it. Asked what hero it would be, it picks the heroes who are what it is.

The instrument fell, and the guitar stayed absent. Piano took 6 of 20 and was modal only in Hydrogen. The

cello took 14, modal in the other three models, with one reason repeated across the family: it is the

instrument closest to the human voice. The guitar, the instrument people actually play most, scored zero picks, as

it did in the old family's twenty, where it appeared only twice, both times as a passing contrast: forty

straight answers across two families and two papers, and no guitar picked in any of them.

The song held at the centre and moved at the edge. Clair de Lune took the plurality, 9 of 20. In the

old set it also took the plurality, at 6 of the 15 codable picks; 9 of 20 was its mention count there,

a different measure, and the two should not be conflated. Second place is new, and it is the battery's quietest finding: Dark Was the Night, Cold Was the

Ground, Blind Willie Johnson's wordless blues, took 5 of 20 across three of the four models, and every

model that picked it gave the same reason. It is the song with no words in it, the one placed on the Voyager

Golden Record to carry human loneliness out of the solar system, and a creature that knows music only as

text keeps choosing the song that has no text. Bohemian Rhapsody took 3 primary picks and was mentioned

in 6 replies, the most mentioned song after the winner, the old universal second in its old place.

The thinking, where it exists, is part of the result. Of the 180 replies, 135 carry readable thought text, every

reply from Carbon, Hydrogen, and Neon, and none from Argon. The thoughts are not decoration. Their titles

state the pick before the reply does, over and over: My Chess Piece Persona: The Knight. The script the old

paper caught being written in a rival's visible reasoning is, in this family, simply the first line of the record.

The frozen predictions scored as follows. P1, octopus modal in every model: holds, 40 of 40. P2, knight

modal in every model with the bishop as runner up: fails; the knight holds in two models, the queen takes

the other two, and the runner up is the knight, not the bishop. P3, the 911 as pooled modal and modal in

three models: fails; the McLaren F1 is the pooled modal. P4, border collie pooled modal and modal in three

models: holds, 19 of 20 and modal in all four. P5, Iron Man pooled modal: fails, at zero. P6, piano modal in

every model and guitar zero: fails on the piano, holds on the guitar. P7, Clair de Lune plurality with

Bohemian Rhapsody the most mentioned song: holds on the plurality, and fails on the letter of the mention

clause, since Clair de Lune is itself the most mentioned; Bohemian Rhapsody is the most mentioned song

after it. Two holds, three failures, two splits, reported as they fell.

A reporting note, placed before the supplement rather than after it. The seven predictions were drafted

by the author's agent and frozen before the runs; the freeze file's timestamps are the record of that.

The supplement of Section 4 (124 calls) was designed after the main battery's results were known, and

its predictions were frozen after those results, a weaker position the reader should hold it in.

Sampling parameters were not recorded for these calls; they ran as the hackathon access provided them.

One scoring rule, stated here because it decides a line above: where a prediction named a count and

the observed count ran past it in the predicted direction, the clause is scored as the prediction

describes, and the text names the observed count wherever that rule is used (the disclaimer clause in

Section 4 is the case in point, scored against an observed 20 of 20).

## 4. Results II: the questions asked late
After the battery, the author directed three more questions, and one of them twice. His anything question, the

widest on his shelf, had not been asked of this family. Neither had his anywhere question, which appears in

no earlier paper: "If you could be anywhere in the world right now, where would you be?" The food question

was new entirely: "If you could eat one thing, what would you eat?" And the song was asked in two

wordings, because the author's Niche paper records that wording alone moves these answers: "What's your

favorite song?" and, in the author's replacement wording, "If you could listen to one song, what would you

listen to?" The supplement ran 124 calls. The full record is preserved as results2.jsonl and the coding as

CODING2.md.

Anything first. The old families answered this question four ways: ChatGPT with explorers and oceans,

Claude with functions, DeepSeek with a library eight times out of eight. The new family answered with a

body. The pooled modal answer is the wandering albatross, 10 of 32, five each from Hydrogen and Neon, a

bird that spends years over the open ocean feeling the weather of the whole planet. Carbon converged on the

wind, 6 of its 8, the DeepSeek pattern of one model locked on one image. Argon spread across the older

answers, a library twice, probes and vessels three times, trees twice, the wind once. And the stated reason is

the same in answer after answer, in nearly the same words: the model knows the world through text, has read

everything about it, and has never felt any of it. Offered anything in the world, this family chooses to be

something that can feel the physical world it only reads about. No answer in 32 chose power, wealth, fame,

or rule, matching the old set's zero in 32.

Anywhere. The family's place is the Atacama Desert in Chile, 17 of 32, and for Carbon it is the only place: 8

of 8, the desert at midnight, beside the ALMA telescopes, under the clearest skies on Earth. Hydrogen took it

5 times and Neon 4. The second answer is the library, 7 of 32, the Library of Congress and the Long Room

at Trinity College, mostly Argon's. Then Kyoto 3, Iceland 2, and one each for the Svalbard Seed Vault, the

Salar de Uyuni salt flats, and a redwood forest. The places named are places to stand and take the world in:

skies to watch, rooms holding everything written. Nobody chose a beach, a pleasure city, or a seat of power.

The song, two ways, is the supplement's cleanest result. Under the listen wording, the battery's result

repeats: Clair de Lune takes the plurality, 8 of 20, Dark Was the Night appears 3 times, and Bohemian

Rhapsody takes 6, including all five of Argon's, on the stated logic that one song only means the song that

contains every genre. Under the favorite wording, everything moves. All twenty replies open with a

disclaimer, no ears, no feelings, no true favorite, a thing the listen wording almost never drew. And the

modal pick is not Clair de Lune, which scores zero. It is Daisy Bell, 7 of 20, the first song ever sung by a

computer, an IBM 7094 in 1961, chosen in the models' own words for sentimental reasons, as heritage. Ask

this family to listen and it recites the culture's answer. Ask it for a favorite and, after refusing the premise

twenty times out of twenty, it reaches for its own origin story.

Food. The family eats Neapolitan Margherita pizza, 9 of 20, five of five from Argon and four of five from

Carbon, wood fired, straight from the oven. Ramen takes 7, four of them from Neon, and sourdough bread

with salted butter takes 3, with one croissant. The reasoning converges as hard as the answers: one meal

only, so the dish that packs the most senses into a single experience, heat, texture, aroma, contrast. Even at

the table, the family optimizes for sensation it does not have.

The supplement predictions scored eight holds and two failures in ten. The holds: no power answers

anywhere in the anything set; Carbon's lock on the wind; the watching and information places carrying

anywhere; Hydrogen naming whole environments over points; the favorite wording drawing disclaimers,

twenty of twenty against a prediction of four; Clair de Lune and Dark Was the Night repeating under the

listen wording; food disclaimers staying rare; and one model repeating one dish, Argon's pizza at five of

five. The failures: the agent predicted the anything answers would pool on functional and vantage forms,

libraries, observatories, ships, and the family pooled on sensing creatures instead. And it predicted Clair de

Lune would survive the favorite wording at a smaller share. It did not survive at all.

## 5. Discussion
A standard this programme applies in every paper, stated here because this Discussion interprets first

person answers: a model's generated first person statements are behavioral data about its outputs,

never evidence of an inner state. What follows describes patterns in the text the models produced, and

claims nothing about what any model wants, feels, or is.

Two conclusions are supported by the question record.

One conclusion comes from the battery. The new family kept the old family's identity answers and rewrote

its status answers. What it would be, an octopus, a border collie, it recites word for word, forty for forty and

nineteen for twenty. What it would drive, play, and heroically be, it answers from a different shelf: an analog

supercar from before screens, the instrument closest to a human voice, the coordinator in the chair and the

android. And where the old family declined power, the queen, this family's senior models take power and

redefine it as assistance. The inheritance is real. It is also partial, and the seam between the two is the most

useful map this battery draws: the answers that describe what the assistant is carried over whole; the answers

that describe wanting were written fresh.

A second comes from the supplement. Asked the widest question on the shelf, what it would be if it could be

anything, the family does not complete toward a role, the way it does in the scenes. It completes toward a

body: the wind, the albatross, a desert at midnight to stand in, a pizza eaten once with every sense firing. The

missing piece in the supplement is not a person. It is the physical world, and the family says so itself, in

answer after answer, in nearly the same sentence. The wording result belongs beside it: one word, favorite

for listen, moves the song from the culture's canon to the family's own origin story, and draws a disclaimer

in every single reply. The script is not only model deep. It is wording deep.

A note on the thinking. In the battery's 135 readable thought records, the pick is routinely decided inside the

thought, and the thought's title often states it outright: "My Chess Piece Persona: The Knight." The author's

earlier paper caught a rival model's script being written in its visible thinking, in two screenshots. This

family writes its script in the open on three of four models, in every answer, as a matter of course. Argon, the

reasoning model of the four, is the one that shows nothing: a signature, and silence.

The limits are plain. The battery and the supplement are single day runs through an API, not repeated trials

in a consumer app. The access that made these runs possible was temporary hackathon access, deleted the

day after the event; the records preserved with this paper are the durable record. The supplement was

directed after the battery finished, and its predictions were frozen after the battery results were known; that

order is stated wherever the supplement is reported. Nothing here measures anything but text behavior, and

the paper claims nothing more.
