# To a Pulp: How the People Making Games and Movies Changed, and What They Did to the Work
**Abdelrahman El Sayad**

## Abstract
Twenty years ago the biggest games and films in the world were built by one kind of team, for one kind of

audience, under one set of rules. Today they are built by different people, under different rules, and the work

shows it. This paper argues a chain of six links. The hiring pipeline changed. The teams changed. The new

makers build through their own eyes and put themselves, their identities, and their politics into franchises

they inherited instead of serving the vision those franchises carried. The products changed in ways anyone

can point to on screen. A captured critic layer protects the change and punishes the people who object to it.

And when the audience walks away, the audience is named as the problem. Every link is evidenced from the

public record: the makers' own words, the owners' own reports, a patent, a blacklist described by the man it

happened to, and the score tables where critics and players grade the same work and land in different worlds.

Where the record cuts against the argument, the record is printed too. Grand Theft Auto VI sold enormously

and scored a 99 with critics. This paper does not claim it failed. It claims something narrower and harder to

kill: the game was transformed, the room that graded it was curated, and the crown was handed out before

the audience was allowed to grade it at all. When the audience verdict opened, it answered 3.7 against the

critics' 99.

## 1. The claim and the method
The claim is not that games and movies got worse in some way no one can measure. Parts of it can be

measured, and this paper measures what it can. The claim is about who makes the work now, what they say

they are doing, and what the work became after they took it over.

The method is simple. A claim counts as evidence here if it comes from a named source on the public

record: a developer speaking in an interview, a studio or its owner speaking in an official document, a

shipped game or film whose content can be checked, a patent filing, or a score table anyone can open. A

claim counts as argument when it is reasoning built on top of that evidence, and the paper says so where it

happens. Claims that circulated widely in commentary videos but failed checking were cut, and the cutting is

part of the method. A paper that prints one false exhibit hands its enemies the knife to kill the true ones. The

evidence base is thirty video transcripts from gaming and film commentators, checked claim by claim

against the public record. What survived checking is what follows.

## 2. Link one: the pipeline changed
Before a team can change, the way teams get built has to change. That is the first link, and it is the best

documented one, because the industry wrote it down itself.

Start with the consultancy layer. Sweet Baby Inc. is a narrative consultancy hired by game studios. In its

own words, on its own site, its mission is "to tell better, more empathetic stories while diversifying and

enriching the video games industry." Its service description for authenticity and sensitivity work reads: "We

approach sensitivity by working with your existing narrative and documentation to bring out its natural

authenticity and emotional resonance. We don't look for red flags, we highlight green ones." Its own project

listing for God of War Ragnarök describes its work as "Narrative and Character Consultation, with a focus

on representation." Wikipedia records that the company consulted on Ragnarök, including work on the

character Angrboda, and that its chief executive founded the company "to promote the advancement of

women and marginalized groups in the video game industry."

None of that is an accusation. It is a sales pitch, printed by the seller. A layer of firms now exists between the

studio and the shipped story whose stated product is representation. Studios pay for it. That is a structural

fact about how games get written in this era, and it did not exist when the franchises in this paper were built.

The consultancy's chief executive, Kim Belair, told Kotaku in March 2024 that the perspective "is never that

we're coming in and injecting diversity," and that companies come to the firm asking to make a character

"more representative and more interesting." Take her at her word and the link still holds: the demand comes

from inside the studios. The pipeline produces makers who want this service, and the service exists to serve

them. The layer is real either way.

Belair had described the method herself years earlier, on a conference stage, before the argument about her

company existed. In 2019 she gave a talk at the Game Developers Conference, "Now You See Me:

Representation as Innovation," advising creatives in AAA studios on how to win approval and budget for

consultants, sensitivity readers, risk assessment, and research: "If you're a creative working in AAA, which I

did for many, many years, put this stuff up to your higher-ups. And if they don't see the value in what you're

asking for when you ask for consultants, when you ask for research, go have a coffee with your marketing

team and just terrify them with the possibility of what's going to happen if they don't give you what you

want." The qualification came in the next breath, and this paper prints it with the quote: "I say that out loud

as a joke, but it's actually very, very true." Her stated reasoning was practical: marketing teams exist to

mitigate harm and keep sentiment positive, and marketing budgets can pay for the work, so marketing is the

department to persuade. Read at the strength the record supports, the mechanism she describes is leverage

through the fear of backlash, carried to the one department whose job is to fear it, and offered as advice to

working developers. The joke framing is hers. So is the sentence after it. By August 2024 she was reciting

the caricature of herself on stage as a joke, opening her XOXO Festival talk with a sarcastic self

introduction: "I am the crazed CEO of Sweet Baby Inc, the DEI-obsessed censorship mafia who is currently

ruining and woke-ifying all of the video games that you've ever played."

Above the consultancies sit the owners, and the owners publish their intentions. Embracer Group, one of the

largest owners in games, stated in its annual report for 2023/24 that its sustainability goals include doubling

"the number of female Managing Directors/Studio Heads by 2025 compared with the base year 2021/22."

That is a hiring target for the top of the studio, set by the owner, in an official report. Whatever any

individual hire deserved, the target itself is a mandate, and mandates are how pipelines change on purpose

rather than by drift.

The pipeline also decides who gets in at the moments that matter. In 2020, at Disney Investor Day,

Lucasfilm president Kathleen Kennedy hired writer and director Justin Simien to lead a Lando series for Star

Wars. Simien went on to direct Disney's Haunted Mansion, which grossed 117.5 million dollars worldwide

against a 150 million dollar budget, with Deadline putting Disney's loss at 117 million dollars. The Lando

series was never produced. By July 2023 Simien was asking in public: "Am I too black? Am I too queer?"

The hire was announced on the biggest stage the company had. The work never appeared. The paper returns

to Simien's audience numbers later; here the point is only the pipeline: who gets handed a franchise, at what

moment, and for what stated reasons.

There is one more pipeline document, and it is not about identity at all. In 2017 the United States granted

Activision a patent, filed in 2015, for a system whose engine "arranges matches to influence" purchases,

matching "a more expert/marquee player with a junior player" so the junior player will want to buy what the

expert is using. Activision said on the record that the patent was exploratory and "has not been

implemented" in a game, and this paper prints that denial with the claim. The patent shows what the modern

pipeline optimizes for: not the match, not the fantasy, but the purchase the match can be arranged to

produce. The old Call of Duty sold skill. The new one patented the arrangement of envy. Section five returns

to what that series became.

## 3. Link two: the teams changed
The second link is the change in who is actually in the room. This paper states plainly what it has and what it

does not have on this link.

What it has now is the credits count the design called for, and it is in the evidence file. The rule is the same

for both games: count every unique person named anywhere in the full credits listing, every department, the

cast, and the licensed music included. Grand Theft Auto IV, in its fullest credits listing, names 2,791 people,

and that figure already includes its cast and its entire song by song music roll. Grand Theft Auto VI has no

usable credits listing in the databases yet, one day after release, so its count is taken from Rockstar's own

published credits. There, the development departments alone list 4,652 names, counted directly from that

page for this paper; six names repeat across departments, leaving 4,646 individual developers. That count

leaves out the cast, the score, the songs, the outside studios, and the special thanks entirely. Counted the

same way, the full official page names 13,391 people in total, though its media roll credits songs name by

name at a granularity the 2008 transcription never attempted, which is why the development only

comparison is the honest one. The development side of the sixth game, with its cast and its music not

counted at all, is already about two thirds larger than the entire credited team of the fourth game, music and

all. The shape changed with the size. The 2008 roll is organized by studio, four Rockstar studios named in

blocks. The 2026 roll is one list for the whole company, organized by department, and it contains

departments with no counterpart in 2008, among them platform services at 286 names and performance

production at 109.

One name in the 2008 roll is missing from the 2026 roll entirely, and it is the name that wrote the series. Dan

Houser co wrote Grand Theft Auto IV with Rupert Humphries. In the official credits of Grand Theft Auto VI

his name appears zero times: not in the writing, not in the thanks, nowhere. The exit is a matter of record.

Houser took an extended break in the spring of 2019, after Red Dead Redemption 2 shipped. In February

2020 Take Two announced the departure in a short filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission: the

break, the gratitude, and a last day, March 11, 2020. No reason was stated, and Houser made no public

comment. The filing came eighteen months after Jason Schreier's reporting for Kotaku documented a culture

of crunch at Rockstar, reporting set off by Houser's own remark that he and a small team had worked 100

hour weeks on Red Dead Redemption 2. Take Two shares fell about five percent the day the departure was

announced. Houser registered a new company, Absurd Ventures, in February 2021 and announced it in 2023,

and in September 2025 he confirmed that Grand Theft Auto VI is the first game in the series he did not

write. Why he went has never been stated by either side, and this paper does not invent a reason. The

author's reading is that the silence is itself the document: the writer of the series was let out quietly and then

written out completely. That reading is labelled as his.

What it does have is the owner's stated intention to change the composition of leadership, quoted in the last

section, and the testimony of the handoff itself, in the words of the people who received the franchises from

the people who built them.

When Volition rebooted Saints Row in 2022, the studio's development director, Jim Boone, explained the

new game by dismissing the old ones: "We love [the old Saints Row games], but we also recognise those

games are of a time. They made sense within that era, and we were able to do things that felt good back

then. But that tone is not something that we feel like we want to do today." An associate user experience

designer on the reboot, Kenzie Lindgren, was twenty three at the time and seven years old when the first

Saints Row shipped. She told interviewers: "I didn't necessarily grow up with the franchise, but there's a lot

of things about the old Saints Row that I'm not necessarily a fan of."

Read those two quotes together and the handoff is described by the people who performed it. The franchise

passed to a generation that did not grow up with it, does not necessarily like it, and was hired to make

something else under its name. That is not a smear. It is a job description, stated by the people holding the

job.

The room also gets replaced mid project when it suits the new direction. Forspoken shipped in 2023 with

famous names on its story credits. One of them, Gary Whitta, said on the record in February 2023: "I never

wrote an actual line of dialogue... but that's literally the only thing I know for sure of mine that's in the

game." Square Enix, he said, told the original writers: "We're going to start over to completely reboot the

story." The writers whose names sold the game did not write the game. The team on the box and the team in

the room were two different teams.

Public perception of the team change has its own evidence, handled here as perception and nothing more.

Two photographs circulate in the debate. One is a genuine photograph of the early Grand Theft Auto team,

about two dozen men around a pool table in a small office, from the DMA era. The other, endlessly shared

as "the staff working on GTA 6," is a collage of photographs from Rockstar women's events, with a Red

Dead Redemption 2 banner visible in the large group shot that dates it to a staff event around 2018. It is not

a roster of the GTA VI team, which numbers in the thousands across many studios, and this paper does not

present it as one. The commentator Andy Pants Gaming claims he assembled the meme himself. What the

collage proves is narrower and still real: the change in who the public believes makes these games became

so total that a meme built from event photographs reads, to millions of people, as a plausible staff photo.

Perception is not staffing. But a franchise does not acquire that perception by accident.

## 4. Link three: they put themselves in
This is the center of the paper, and it needs no commentary, because the strongest exhibits are confessions.

The makers say, in their own words, that the work comes out of themselves: their families, their identities,

their experiences, their politics. The old doctrine was to serve the franchise and its audience. The new

doctrine, stated proudly and in print, is that the maker is the source.

Exhibit one is the biggest game in the world. Alexa D'Agostino is a writer at Rockstar. In October 2026, in

LOVE Magazine, she described writing Lucia, the female lead of Grand Theft Auto VI:

"When it came to writing Lucia, I thought of the women I know. My mother was an immigrant who grew up

in Newark, New Jersey, close to Lucia's hometown of Liberty City. I thought about the way she raised me:

rowdy, kind, but not to take any shit. I related a lot to Lucia, and writing her came very naturally to me.

There are many badass women like her, just as there are many men like Jason... That's how you get a woman

that can stand tall next to the likes of Arthur Morgan and Niko Bellic."

The lead of the largest crime franchise on Earth was built out of the writer's own mother and the writer's

own life, and the writer says so in a fashion magazine. No critic has to allege self insertion here. It is

announced.

Exhibit two is the owner of the studios saying the quiet part in an annual report. Embracer Group, again in

its own words: "Embracer Group believes that a more diverse and inclusive workplace will create more

diverse and inclusive games and entertainment." And: "Embracer Group wants to develop games and

entertainment for everyone, regardless of background, gender, age, or ability." The same report says the

group "has focused on making our products mirror their audiences even better." The sentence is the whole

mechanism in one line, printed by the mechanism's owner: change who is in the workplace and the games

change to match. The pipeline is not a side effect of the product. By the owner's own account, the pipeline is

the cause and the product is the effect.

Exhibit three is a game director describing her own identity as the game's content. Corinne Busche directed

Dragon Age: The Veilguard for BioWare. In September 2024 she said: "As a queer trans woman, I have a

perspective on the games that not everyone has. Dragon Age has long been a place where LGBTQIA+ folks

can see people like themselves, represented respectfully. It's inherently very queer, and it's such a rare thing

for marginalized communities to have representation where we feel proud and powerful in how we are

depicted... I hope we can be a safe place for our queer players." To Inverse that December: "I'm an openly

queer, trans woman. It shaped everything about who I am, and it's been the source of a lot of joy, a lot of

difficulty and perspective." She also insisted, on the record, that every companion in the game is specifically

pansexual. The shipped game matches the description: its character creator offers top surgery scars, and a

companion's story line is a coming out narrative built around an apology ritual. The director's identity was

not a private fact about the director. By her own account it was the design brief.

Exhibit four is older and shows the pattern is about the maker, not only about one politics. Neil Druckmann

built The Last of Us Part II around revenge, and he has said where it came from. On seeing footage of the

2000 Ramallah lynching as a teenager, he told the Washington Post in 2020: "It was the cheering that was

really chilling to me. In my mind, I thought 'Oh, man, if I could just push a button and kill all these people

that committed this horrible act, I would make them feel the same pain that they inflicted on these people.'"

He told Haaretz in 2023 that the game was inspired by "my feeling towards a cycle of violence that I've

experienced as a child growing up in Israel, growing up in the West Bank specifically." The game's two

warring factions were partly drawn from that conflict. A player's twenty five hours inside that world is, by

the maker's own account, a walk through the maker's own wound. Players who objected to where the story

forced them were not disagreeing with a plot. They were declining someone else's therapy, and the maker

had never hidden that therapy was the product.

Exhibit five is the rule stated as a rule. The Saints Row reboot's writer, Jennifer Campbell, described the

team's writing doctrine in an interview: the rule is "making sure that you're punching up, not punching

down." A comedy franchise built on punching everything in sight was handed to a room that began from a

rule about which directions jokes are allowed to travel. The rule is political on its face, and it was applied

before a single scene was written.

Exhibit six comes from film, and it is the purest case in the file. In The Batman of 2022, the mayoral

candidate Bella Reál was not in the original script. In an interview clip preserved in the evidence, a speaker

describes what director Matt Reeves told her: the character "wasn't in the original script. He was inspired by

AOC and Stacey Abrams and that whole group of... women of color that really started to come in, what was

that, 2016?" A director took sitting politicians of his own politics, built a character on them, and wrote her

into an inherited franchise whose script did not contain her. That is self insertion with the serial numbers

showing.

Exhibit seven is a director announcing her politics as her film's origin. Maggie Gyllenhaal, writer and

director of The Bride!, said in an interview clip that she became a director "the morning that Trump was first

elected," and described the film as her attempt to give voice to what Mary Shelley could not express. The

film cost 90 million dollars to produce and returned 24 million at the box office. Olivia Wilde, promoting

Don't Worry Darling, stated that she modeled the film's cult leader villain on Jordan Peterson. In both cases

the maker said, before anyone could accuse them, that the politics came first and the film was built to carry

it.

Exhibit eight is the doctrine stated as a defence. In the Eddas, Angrboda is an adult giantess, mother of

Fenrir, the Midgard serpent, and Hel. God of War Ragnarök reimagined her as a young Black teenager, and

when the objection came, the game's narrative director, Matt Sophos, answered it in a public thread. He

described the design himself: "Angrboda in our game is a young black woman." He denied the studio had

caved to anyone: "I can count on 2 fingers the # of notes we've gotten for story changes to 2018 + GoW

Ragnarok combined. This was not one of them." And he stated the rule underneath this whole section: "As a

writer, your job is to tell stories that are compelling & relatable. You bring pieces of yourself to stories and

your opinions shine through. So writing is always political—it's just usually only called that if the politics

aren't agreed with." The maker is the source, again, this time as a point of pride under attack.

Exhibit nine is a design standard announced across two games. Mortal Kombat's female fighters were, in the

2011 game, the series' visual baseline: exaggerated, in bikini style costumes. In 2015, presenting Mortal

Kombat X, production manager Spiro Anagnostakos said the mantra was realism, and that "the same thing

applies to the proportions where we try to bring things back in per se to where they should be." When

another developer added that the women would look "more like women do," he answered: "Exactly." In

2019, art director Steve Beran described Mortal Kombat 11: "Our design is just getting more mature and

respectful. You're not going to wear a bikini to a fight. You're not going to be showing so much skin." And:

"We don't have bathing suit fighters, and I think that's fine. If people are disappointed, I don't regret making

that change by any means." No one alleged a hidden motive. The studio announced the new sensibility as an

improvement on the old one, in its own words, twice, four years apart.

The pattern across the exhibits is one sentence in different mouths: I put myself in. My mother. My identity.

My childhood. My politicians. My election morning. Sections two and three showed who got the jobs. This

section showed what they said they would do with them. The next section shows the work.

## 5. Link four: the products changed
If the first three links are true, the work itself has to show it. It does. This section goes franchise by

franchise, and it starts with the honest version of the biggest case, including the numbers that cut against this

paper.

**Grand Theft Auto.** Grand Theft Auto IV, in 2008, was a revenge story about an immigrant killer named

Niko Bellic. Critics scored it 98. Players scored it 8.3. Grand Theft Auto V, in 2013, scored 97 with critics

and 8.2 with players. On a single scale, those are gaps of about fifteen points: 98 against 83, and 97 against

82. The modern splits run twenty to forty five points, two to three times wider. The two publics disagreed for

twenty years, inside the same world.

Grand Theft Auto VI, released November 19, 2026, is built around a couple, Jason and Lucia, and around

their relationship. The documented mechanics of its opening hours include house repairs, groceries, flowers,

and relationship maintenance by text and call. Launch coverage in The Guardian described it as the first title

in the series to encourage empathy. The streamer Asmongold, playing it on stream, said: "This game's called

Grand Theft Auto, and you're out there doing chores, getting laundry." In the launch debate he called the

game an actual cuck simulator and said it felt written by a woman, a female fantasy. Those are his words and

his opinion, labelled that way. But the content underneath the opinion is checkable, and it checks out.

Solicitation of prostitutes was a feature of every mainline Grand Theft Auto from GTA III in 2001 onward. It

is not in GTA VI. Rockstar confirmed the removal in its own words: "GTAVI is a game about a couple in a

bind, lovers on the run... Many aspects of previous titles are not present." Games journalist Jason Schreier

summarized the shipped rules in one line: "You can't kiss anyone except your partner." A crime fantasy that

ran for twenty five years on the promise that the player could do anything now ships with a fidelity system.

The removal has a history, and the history is a campaign. In 2014, a petition carrying some 45,000 signatures asked Target in Australia to withdraw Grand Theft Auto V, not for its violence in general but for sexual violence against women in particular, and Target and Kmart in Australia pulled the game from their shelves. The mechanic underneath the petition was real: a player could pay a sex worker, kill her, and take the money back from the body, an incentive the game itself had built in. Watching a video essay on this history in November 2026, the streamer Asmongold drew the line to the present himself: Grand Theft Auto VI, he said, removed prostitution from the series for the first time since GTA III because of that 2014 petition. The essay he was watching made the wider point. Children have never existed in a Grand Theft Auto game at all, not because the fiction forbids them but because a game that let players kill children would draw an adults only rating, and an adults only game cannot be sold on the major platforms. The lines in this series were never drawn by the fiction. They were drawn by ratings boards, retailers, and petitions, around some classes of people and not others.

Grand Theft Auto VI draws a new line of the same kind, this one inside the game. Cygnus, a museum curator in Vice City and, on the actor's account, the first nonbinary character in the series, cannot be harmed by the player. Jesse James Rose, who played the role, wrote in a November 2026 Instagram post that Rockstar assured them players would not be able to inflict violence on the character, in their words, "to mitigate anti-trans violence." Polygon and Eurogamer reported the post. Rockstar has not confirmed it, and no gameplay recording has yet settled how the protection works, so it is recorded here as what it is: the actor's account of a deliberate protection, extended to one identity, in a game world that otherwise lets the player harm nearly anyone.

The transformation is not alleged. The maker announced it as fidelity to the story it wanted to tell: the self

insertion doctrine of section four, operating as a design decision.

Now the counter facts, printed in full. GTA VI scored 99 on Metacritic from 152 critic reviews, every one of

them positive. Preorders were reported at roughly 1.6 billion dollars, around 17 million copies, before

launch. This paper does not claim the game failed, because it did not. The claim is that the product was

transformed and that the grade was awarded by a room whose membership is the subject of section six. One

more fact belongs in the open. Metacritic opened user reviews for GTA VI at noon Pacific time on

November 20, 2026, and this paper pulled the first reading that same afternoon. The audience answered 3.7,

rated generally unfavorable, from the first 157 ratings: 40 positive, 23 mixed, 94 negative. Set against the

critics' 99 on a single scale, that is a gap of 62 points, the widest in this paper's table. The reading is early,

small, and self selected, and it will move as more ratings land. Two contexts belong beside it: the

critics' 99 rests on 152 advance reviews, and the day one user ratings were gathered while a download

code boycott was urging low scores from players who had not been given codes. It is recorded here as what it is, the first

verdict the audience was allowed to give, on a score the platform had already been policing: Metacritic

removed fake user ratings posted before launch earlier that week.

The road to that launch is part of the record, and it shows what rode on this game. Rockstar confirmed

development in February 2022. That September, a seventeen year old member of the hacking group

Lapsus$, Arion Kurtaj, broke into Rockstar's internal systems from a hotel room, while on bail for earlier

hacks, and posted about ninety clips of the unfinished game. From inside the company's own Slack he

delivered the ultimatum: if Rockstar did not contact him on Telegram within twenty four hours, he would

start releasing the source code. Take Two shares fell more than six percent in premarket trading the next

morning. A jury later found that Kurtaj had carried out the acts, and in December 2023 he received an

indefinite hospital order; in November 2026, days before launch, he pleaded guilty. The first trailer, in

December 2023, took the Guinness record for a video game reveal, with 90.4 million views in twenty four

hours. The game was then delayed twice before it shipped, thirteen years after Grand Theft Auto V.

The launch itself broke a second thing the series had kept since 2001: the disc. Grand Theft Auto VI shipped

with no disc at all. The boxes on store shelves contain a download code, and The Hollywood Reporter

confirmed there are no plans to print discs, at launch or later. The backlash sits in the launch coverage itself:

players announcing boycotts in the local press, shop owners refusing the release, and the chief executive of

GameStop, Ryan Cohen, arguing in public that buyers should be able to resell, trade, and lend what they

paid for. In October 2026 the fight turned criminal. An anonymous leaker calling itself Cyberleek, which had

been posting alleged footage since August, threatened to release a complete playable build of the game, a

build no one has proved exists, unless Take Two released the game on disc and issued a public apology. The

threat hung on a cryptocurrency token target that was never met, and no build appeared. A company that had

survived one teenager emptying its servers now faced a second extortion built entirely on how it had chosen

to sell the game.

**Halo.** The original Halo games were heroic, military, and built on biblical architecture: a Covenant,

Prophets, a Flood, an Ark. The case in the transcripts is a content case, presented here as the commentator's

documented reading. Halo 4 opens with Dr. Halsey, the scientist who built the Spartan program, interrogated

as a war criminal, and a Spartan's first line to the Master Chief is "I thought you'd be taller." The argument in

the evidence is that the 343 era games diminished the Chief, recast the military that created him as the

villain, and stripped out the religious frame, while Halo Infinite shipped Pride, Women's Day, and Black

History Month armor cosmetics on schedule during a launch missing core features. The scores show no

blowout: 87 with critics, 7.9 with players. The Halo claim is about direction, not rejection, and the paper

states it at that strength.

**Gears of War.** The original trilogy was built around Marcus Fenix and a squad of soldiers. Gears 5, in 2019,

moved the center of the franchise to Kait Diaz. The scores again show agreement rather than collapse, 84

with critics and 8.1 with players. The Gears case is the swap itself: a franchise built for a male audience,

around a male lead, handed to a female lead as a deliberate new direction. Whether any single swap is good

or bad is taste. That the swaps form a pattern is the subject of the film catalogue below.

**Call of Duty.** The patent of section two hangs over this franchise. What the audience can see is the surface:

a military shooter whose store filled with crossover costumes, Nicki Minaj, the Ninja Turtles, Beavis and

Butt-Head, Seth Rogen, until the uniform the series was built on became one costume among cartoons. The

commentator's player count claims describe Steam concurrency falling from half a million around Warzone

2.0 in 2022 to roughly sixty thousand after Black Ops 6, and those figures are his claims, labelled as such.

What is not a claim is the score table: Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III stands at 56 with critics and 2.6 with

players. Both publics turned on it, and the players turned harder.

**Kingdom Come.** This is the cleanest before and after in the file: the same studio made both games, and an

owner change sits between them. In 2015, Warhorse Studios' YouTube channel answered a question about

same sex romance by stating that Henry was not homosexual and is completely straight. A Warhorse

community manager later clarified that he wrote that comment himself, without input from studio head

Daniel Vávra, and blamed the first game's straight Henry on budget constraints. The exact sentence quoted

in commentary videos, "he is straight and white and male from Bohemia," could not be located word for

word in the record, and this paper does not use it. In 2019, Embracer Group bought Warhorse. In February

2025, Kingdom Come: Deliverance II shipped with an optional romance between Henry and Hans Capon,

which Vávra defended on the record: "We made the gay community happy and gave them the CHOICE to

be themselves." The studio that once stated its hero was completely straight now sells his romance as a

feature, under the owner whose annual report says diverse workplaces create diverse games. Each step is

documented. The reader can decide what moved.

**The remakes.** The clearest laboratory is the remake, because the original sits next to the new version and

anyone can compare them. The Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion Remastered, released in April 2025, rebuilt the

2006 game and changed its vocabulary at the first screen: character creation no longer offers Male and

Female but Type 1 and Type 2, Type 1 being the former female body, with the old stat differences moved to

a new Origin choice, and the character edit prompt renamed from Edit Race to Edit Identity. Bethesda and

Virtuos have never explained the change. When a mod restoring the words male and female, and changing

nothing else, was uploaded to Nexus Mods the day after launch, Nexus removed it and banned its creator;

PC Gamer reported that the ban cited a policy against files that remove "gender identity including pronouns

from a game that have been intentionally put in place by a game developer." Within two days Nexus

reversed itself and let the mod stand, saying the removal had been for the uploader's behavior, and that "our

platform exists to support creative expression in modding, not to serve as a battleground for broader cultural

or political debates."

The design changes run deeper than the labels, and they are documented by direct comparison with the

shipped 2006 game, most completely in a side by side video catalogue by the creator Angelikatosh. Female

clothing pieces that were off shoulder, low cut, or figure fitting were covered or redesigned. The Huntsman's

vest, which bared the midriff on women, now uses the full vest form the male version wears. Branwen, an

arena trainee, traded a sports bra style top for a warm furry vest while her shirtless male sparring partner

stayed shirtless. The iron, amber, chainmail, and arena armors, which had distinct female fits in 2006, the

steel set was already identical, came back bulky in the male form. The statues of Meridia and Azura were

toned down and covered. The Spider Daedra, servants of Mephala, the prince of seduction, have visibly

smaller chests. The same catalogue flags the inconsistencies itself: the Molag Bal statue and the Anvil

mermaid statue were left alone, and the remaster's Flame Atronach went the other way entirely, redesigned

conspicuously curvy. And the counter fact that governs the whole case: the remaster was a hit, with four

million players in under a week, the best selling game in the United States in April 2025, and nine million

players by July. The claim here is not failure, and it is not proven intent; no one at Bethesda or Virtuos has

stated a motive. The claim is that a twenty year old game's female form was revised in one direction, item by

item, in a product the audience bought anyway.

Capcom's remakes show the same hand, with the maker explaining itself. Dead Rising Deluxe Remaster

removed the original's Erotica photo category, which had awarded points for photographs of women's

cleavage and underwear, and rewrote the quest that required such a shot. Capcom's statement is worth

printing for what it denies: the removal was "not so much as a response to a changing cultural climate," the

company said, but because "it is not an appropriate reward for survival and not a skill required of a journalist

trying to stay alive for the next 72 hours during a zombie apocalypse." The same remaster cut the mentions

of the Viet Cong and communism from the dialogue of Cliff Hudson, a Vietnam veteran. In Resident Evil 4's

remake, Ashley Graham's miniskirt became, in Polygon's words, a skort and a pair of tights, and one of the

original's most quoted lines was cut: where Luis once said, "I see that the president's equipped his daughter

with ballistics too," the remake gives him, "Eeyyy, I see you found your missing Seniorita." In Bloober

Team's Silent Hill 2, Maria's outfit, nearly identical to the one Christina Aguilera wore at the 1999 Teen

Choice Awards, was replaced with a red leather jacket and black dress that Kotaku called "decidedly more

demure"; the original outfit survives inside the remake only as an Easter egg. Angela's redesign, by contrast,

was minimal, a younger face over the same outfit and hair, and this paper states that case at that size.

Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door shows a script revised across three versions. In the 2004 Japanese

original, Vivian's party description reads, "She looks like a girl, but she's really a boy," and her sister taunts

her for calling the group three sisters. The 2004 English version cut every gender reference; the taunt

became an insult about her looks, "PLUG-UGLY," in the same slot. The 2024 remake wrote the point back

in new words: "Truth is, it took me a while to realize I was their sister...not their brother." Nintendo made no

public statement; the change exists only in the shipped text. Tomb Raider I–III Remastered shipped with a

warning in Crystal Dynamics' own words, that the games "contain offensive depictions of people and

cultures rooted in racial and ethnic prejudices. These stereotypes are deeply harmful, inexcusable, and do not

align with our values at Crystal Dynamics," and a promise: "Rather than removing this content, we have

chosen to present it here in its original form, unaltered." Two months later, a patch removed two Lara Croft

posters from the HD mode of The Lost Artefact, a removal mentioned nowhere in the patch notes. Players

found it and objected; Aspyr called the removal inadvertent, and a second patch restored the posters. The

warning said the past would be shown unaltered. The patch record is the footnote.

**The blowouts.** Three games show what happens when the divergence reaches the audience at full strength.

Dragon Age: The Veilguard scored 82 with critics and 4.0 with players. It reached 1.5 million players by the

end of 2024, roughly half of what its publisher EA expected, and EA shut BioWare down in 2026. Saints

Row scored 61 with critics and 3.3 with players, and Volition was closed in 2023. Concord scored 62 with

critics and 1.7 with players, peaked at 697 concurrent players on Steam, was pulled from sale eleven days

after launch, had its servers shut down days later, and its studio, Firewalk, was closed within two months. Its

budget is reported at over 200 million dollars, with an unconfirmed figure of 400 million also circulating,

and this paper prints it at that hedged strength and no stronger. Assassin's Creed Shadows stands at 81 with

critics and 6.1 with players.

Assassin's Creed Shadows deserves its own paragraph because the maker's paper trail is complete. Ubisoft

built the game around two leads, the shinobi Naoe and Yasuke, a real historical figure whose status as a

samurai historians dispute. In Japan, a petition against the portrayal drew some 95,000 signatures. On July

23, 2024, Ubisoft addressed Japanese players directly: "we acknowledge that some elements in our

promotional materials have caused concern within the Japanese community. For this, we sincerely

apologize," and, of the central dispute, "While Yasuke is depicted as a samurai in Assassin's Creed Shadows,

we acknowledge that this is a matter of debate and discussion." The game was then delayed twice. In the

release announcing the first delay, chief executive Yves Guillemot wrote that Ubisoft is "an entertainment
first company, creating games for the broadest possible audience, and our goal is not to push any specific

agenda." The apology, the concession, and the denial of any agenda are all in the maker's own documents.

The game shipped to the split recorded above: 81 with critics, 6.1 with players.

Below the blowouts sits a floor of releases the audience barely attended at all. Dustborn, whose diverse cast

was the subject of a public dispute its director answered by saying no one had forced it on the studio, peaked

at 83 concurrent players on Steam by SteamDB's count; it had the misfortune to launch the same day as

Black Myth: Wukong. Flintlock: The Siege of Dawn peaked between 541 and 648 on Steam depending on

the counter, though its publisher announced 500,000 players across all platforms, Game Pass included, in its

first weeks. Unknown 9: Awakening peaked at 285. Its studio, Reflector, cut 18 percent of its workforce

within a month of launch. In January 2025 its chief executive, Hervé Hoerdt, called the game "the failure of

the studio's ambitious and courageous first project" and wrote that its performance "didn't come near the

company expectations." The follow up project was cancelled, and in November 2026 Bandai Namco

announced Reflector's dissolution, with an expected extraordinary loss of about 21 million euros. Tales of

Kenzera: ZAU peaked at 287 players despite a 76 on Metacritic and 82 percent positive Steam reviews; its

studio, Surgent, put its entire games team on notice of redundancy in October 2024 while it looked for

funding, and coverage of the closure cited an oversaturated genre. Precision matters here as much as

anywhere in this paper: no reputable coverage of any of the four names DEI as the cause of failure. The

studios cite expectations, funding, timelines, and competition, and those explanations stand in the evidence

file beside the numbers. Three of the four carry Sweet Baby Inc. in their writing credits, a fact printed here

as a fact and not as a cause. The claim is narrower than the slogan version. A run of products built under the

new doctrine met audiences so small that the studios did not survive them, and the one the critics liked died

of the same indifference. The pattern is the exhibit.

**Film and television.** The movie side of the pattern is a catalogue, and a catalogue is the right form, because

the argument is the repetition. Twenty one named cases of race swaps, gender flips, and lead role swaps

were assembled from the transcripts. The confirmed core of the list: Ariel, played by Halle Bailey in The

Little Mermaid of 2023. Tinker Bell, played by Yara Shahidi in Peter Pan and Wendy. Snow White, played

by Rachel Zegler in 2025, in a production where Zegler publicly called the 1937 original dated and its prince

a stalker, and where the seven dwarfs were replaced by computer generated creatures after Peter Dinklage

criticized the dwarf portrayal. The Silver Surfer, a male character named Norrin Radd since the 1960s,

replaced by the female Shalla-Bal in The Fantastic Four: First Steps, a film whose producer Grant Curtis

announced in advance that Sue Storm is "arguably the leader of the Fantastic 4," and whose Reed Richards,

Pedro Pascal, said in the speaker's attribution that he loves being led by powerful women. Jim Gordon and

Selina Kyle in The Batman, played by Jeffrey Wright and Zoë Kravitz. Black Cat in the Spider-Noir series.

Severus Snape in the upcoming HBO Harry Potter series, played by Paapa Essiedu. The Doctor in Doctor

Who, played by Ncuti Gatwa. Cleopatra in Netflix's Queen Cleopatra, played by Adele James. Anne Boleyn

in the Channel 5 series, played by Jodie Turner-Smith. Helen of Troy in The Odyssey, played by Lupita

Nyong'o. MJ in the Marvel Spider-Man films, played by Zendaya. Velma Dinkley, voiced and modeled as

South Asian by Mindy Kaling in Velma. The voices of Megatron and Bumblebee in Transformers One. And

the lead role of Incredibles 2, swapped from Bob Parr to Helen Parr, beat for beat the first film with the wife

in the husband's place.

Two entries carry their own warnings, printed with them. The casting of Harvey Dent and Harvey Bullock in

The Batman Part II is an unconfirmed rumor, labelled a rumor by the speaker who reported it. One entry

runs in reverse: Goku, played by the white actor Justin Chatwin in Dragonball Evolution of 2009. And one is

the speakers' own approved exception: Michael Clarke Duncan as Kingpin in Daredevil of 2003, which the

harshest critic in the file concedes works because "the actor fits the role so perfectly." A catalogue that

includes its own exception and its own reverse case is not a tantrum. It is a standard being applied: the swap

is judged against the role, and the modern run of swaps fails that judgment in the audience's eyes, at scale, in

one direction.

The money matches the catalogue where the money can be checked. The Marvels grossed 206.1 million

dollars worldwide, and Deadline estimated its loss at 237 million dollars, the biggest money loser of 2023.

Birds of Prey grossed 205.5 million dollars worldwide against a break even point reported around 250 to

300 million. Its director, Cathy Yan, had directed exactly one prior film, an unreleased independent feature,

when she was handed a production of about 100 million dollars; the essayist in the evidence says plainly, "I

suspect that Kathy Yan might have been a diversity hire," printed here as his suspicion next to the

documented résumé. Chaos Walking grossed 27.1 million dollars against a budget of 100 million, roughly

125 million with reshoots. Ghostbusters of 2016 grossed 229.1 million against a 144 million dollar budget

and lost over 70 million. The Little Mermaid grossed 569.6 million dollars and landed around break even: a

disappointment, not a bomb, and this paper calls it that. Mufasa: The Lion King, which rewrote two brothers

into found family, grossed 722.6 million dollars, and that counter example is printed too. The argument was

never that every changed film loses money. It is that the losses cluster where the audience verdict collapses,

and the audience verdict is the next exhibit.

Television gives the collapse a time series. Dear White People ran four seasons on Netflix. Its Rotten

Tomatoes critic score moved from 95 percent in season one to 67 percent in season four. Its audience score

moved from 61 percent to 52 to 27 to 16. Trailer views fell from 5.1 million for the first season to eighty

thousand for the fourth. The critics held the show up for four years while the audience left in stages, and the

show was renewed through all of it. The essayist who assembled those numbers calls its creator "a typical

trauma dump director" who uses his projects as therapy sessions for his own personal and societal hang ups.

The words are his. The numbers are Rotten Tomatoes'. Both point the same way.

## 6. Link five: the protection
A transformation this large, meeting an audience verdict this clear, should have been corrected by the market

years ago. It was not, because a layer sits between the makers and the market whose job, in practice, is to

grade the work kindly and punish the graders who refuse. The centerpiece of this section is one man,

because he put his name and his dates on the record.

Patrick O'Rourke is a Canadian technology and games journalist who has worked in the field since roughly

2009. On November 15, 2026, four days before Grand Theft Auto VI launched, he posted this:

"Just ahead of GTA VI's release, it feels like a good time to bring up that Rockstar blacklisted me in 2013 for

not giving GTA V an overwhelmingly glowing review. For the record, my review was resoundingly positive,

I just didn't think the game's story compelling as GTA IV's."

In a second post the same day he added the mechanism:

"Just to add some context, I was on Rockstar's media and review copy list back in 2013, when I was

reviewing games for a national Canadian newspaper chain. After my GTA V review was published, I never

heard from Rockstar again."

The review itself is a public document. It ran in Postmedia under the title "Grand Theft Auto V is both

amazing and disappointing." No numeric score for it could be located in the record, and this paper invents

none. Postmedia later purged the review, around 2017; it survives in the Wayback Machine, and O'Rourke

says he still stands by it.

Precision matters here, because this exhibit is strong exactly to the extent that it is stated narrowly. The

claim that Rockstar "blacklisted every journalist that didn't give it a perfect rating" is not O'Rourke's

sentence. It is the streamer Asmongold's gloss on the post, and Asmongold hedged it himself on the same

stream: "So maybe that's true. I mean, maybe it is. I don't know. I think it's possible." What O'Rourke

himself claims is narrower and, for that reason, unkillable: he was on the list, he praised the game, he ranked

its story below the previous game's, and the access ended, permanently, with no explanation, in 2013.

Thirteen years later the room that remained gave the sequel a 99, from 152 reviews, every one positive.

Set that beside the full table, pulled from Metacritic on November 20, 2026. Grand Theft Auto IV: critics 98,

players 8.3. Grand Theft Auto V: critics 97, players 8.2. Halo Infinite: 87 and 7.9. Gears 5: 84 and 8.1.

Assassin's Creed Shadows: 81 and 6.1. Dragon Age: The Veilguard: 82 and 4.0. Saints Row: 61 and 3.3.

Concord: 62 and 1.7. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare III: 56 and 2.6. Grand Theft Auto VI: critics 99, players

3.7.

The pattern is not that critics like everything. Modern Warfare III proves they do not. The pattern is that

where the transformation runs deepest, the two scores split into different worlds, and the critic score is the

one printed in the advertisements. A reader who trusts the top number on Veilguard is reading the verdict of

the room O'Rourke was removed from, in an earlier franchise, for a smaller offense than honesty.

The audience, locked out of the official record, keeps a record of its own. DEI Detective, a catalogue run by

one person who signs as Operator 0, indexes games, films, and series and tags each with the elements it

finds: consultancy credits, pronoun options, redesigns, swaps. Its counts are its own and they conflict: 656

games in a crawl of its index in 2026, more than 1,110 by the operator's later claim. Its percentage figures

are nowhere defined, its source lists sit behind a members wall, and at least one of its analyses carries a

footer reading "Powered by Grok," though the operator says every analysis is reviewed by hand. This paper

cites it as what it is, a community kept index of the change cases, not as a finding. Where its entries overlap

cases checked independently in the evidence file, the independent check governs: the catalogue asserts, as a

finding, the Saga Anderson race swap, a claim that Remedy's game director denied on the record, "It's

absolutely not true," and that this paper's evidence file checked and cut. That is exactly why the catalogue is

cited at this strength and no stronger.

The protection also runs against makers who break ranks, and the dates are short. On September 4, 2021,

John Gibson, president of Tripwire Interactive, the studio he co founded, posted one tweet in favor of the

Texas abortion law, saying he was proud of the Supreme Court for affirming it and that, with so many vocal

peers on the other side, he wanted it on the record that he was a pro life game developer. On September 5,

partner studio Shipwright cancelled its contracts with Tripwire, saying: "the moment you make them a

matter of public discourse, you entangle all of those working for and with you." By September 7, Tripwire

announced Gibson had "stepped down" as chief executive, adding that his comments "disregarded the values

of our whole team, our partners and much of our broader community." One tweet, three days, a founder gone

from his own company.

Scott Cawthon created Five Nights at Freddy's alone. In June 2021 his political donations became public. On

June 12 he posted his answer, declaring himself a Republican, a Christian, and pro life: "I believe in God. I

also believe in equality and in science and in common sense. Despite what some may say, all of those things

can go together. That's not an apology or promise to change, it's the way it's always been. If I get cancelled,

then I get cancelled." On June 16, four days later, he announced his retirement from the series.

Markus Persson, Notch, created Minecraft and sold it to Microsoft for 2.5 billion dollars in 2014. In March

2019 a game update removed the splash text references to him from Minecraft's menus. His name remains in

the game's end credits, and this paper states the correction plainly, because the wider claim that he was

erased from the credits is wrong. He was not invited to the game's tenth anniversary celebration that May. A

Microsoft spokesperson told Variety: "His comments and opinions do not reflect those of Microsoft or

Mojang and are not representative of Minecraft." The man who made the best selling game in history is, by

his buyer's account, not representative of it. Removal from the menus, exclusion from the party, retention in

the credits: that is the narrowed, documented version, and it is enough.

One more account belongs in this section, labelled at its true strength. The commentator Andy Pants Gaming

states that his critical GTA VI video received a manual YouTube copyright claim, about twelve days after

upload, over a five second clip of a Bellamy Brothers song, while creators who used the same song in praise

of the game were not claimed and stayed monetized: "They're not copyrighting the people who glaze the

game but they are copyrighting the people who say bad stuff about their game." This paper has not

independently verified the claim and prints it as his account, no stronger than that.

## 7. Link six: the blame shift
The last link closes the circuit. When the transformed product meets the audience and the audience leaves,

the explanation offered by the makers and their press is never the product. It is the audience. The audience is

too bigoted, too nostalgic, too loud, too small a minority to matter, or simply not the audience the product

was for. The product was for a different audience, one that is always about to arrive.

The film critic The Critical Drinker named the mechanism in the strongest passage in the movie evidence.

The modern audience, he argues, is "a myth, a convenient scapegoat created by writers of these projects to

deflect blame and criticism away from themselves... ultimately the people they're really catering to are

themselves. It's an excuse for them to indulge their own narcissism, to impose their own world view, to undo

the things that they don't like and replace it with stuff they personally want to see."

The makers supply the confirming quotes themselves. Rachel Zegler, asked about the jokes calling her film

the PC Snow White, answered in Vanity Fair: "yeah it is because it needed that," and described her version

as "a refreshing story about a young woman who has a function beyond 'Someday My Prince Will Come'."

The audience that objected was answered not with an argument but with a diagnosis: the old story needed

fixing, and objecting to the fix is the symptom. James Bond producer Barbara Broccoli told Variety: "Bond

is evolving just as men are evolving. I don't know who's evolving at a faster pace." The franchise is fine. It is

men who are behind schedule. Daniel Vávra, defending the Kingdom Come change, reached for the standard

unit of dismissal, the "very small and very loud minority." Volition's official Saints Row account, facing the

backlash to the reboot reveal, posted: "We are not backing down on this game." The studio closed in

2023, the year after the game it would not back down on was released. The audience it would not back down to was the only one it had.

The press layer performs the same move on the audience directly. When players objected that solicitation

had been removed from GTA VI, a Daily Mail headline read on stream framed the removal as a ban meant to

deter incel culture among gamers. The complaint about a changed product was reported as a defect in the

complainers. A man who objects to chores in a crime game is not a customer with a preference. He is a

culture to be deterred.

This is the link that makes the other five permanent. A normal industry reads a 4.0 against an 82, a 1.7

against a 62, a show losing its audience in four measured stages, and changes course. This industry built a

vocabulary in which every one of those numbers is the audience's fault, and a critic layer, section six, that

keeps printing the top number. The product cannot fail. It can only be failed, by the people it was made for,

who are then told it was never made for them at all.

## 8. What this paper does not claim
Three limits, stated plainly. First, commerce: Grand Theft Auto VI made more money before launch than

most franchises make in their lives, Mufasa made 722.6 million dollars, and The Little Mermaid roughly

broke even. The transformation this paper documents is not a universal commercial poison, and any version

of the argument that says so is false. The claim is about content, credit, and the critic layer, and about the

specific titles where the audience verdict collapsed in the open. Second, causation: the composition link of

section three rests in part on argument. The credits count is a headcount. It shows how many people were

credited and how the roll is organized, and correlation between who is hired and what ships is not, by itself,

proof of which caused which. What closes most of that gap is not correlation. It is the makers stating the

intention and the intention matching the product, which is why section four is built from their words. Third,

the audience verdict on the biggest case arrived after this paper's research closed, and the paper has been

corrected by it, exactly as this section promised it would be. GTA VI's user score opened at noon Pacific

time on November 20, 2026, and the first reading, recorded in sections five and six, was 3.7 against the

critics' 99. It widened this paper's central gap rather than narrowing it, to the widest gap in the table. The

caveat stands with it: the reading is early, drawn from a small self selected sample, and the settled score will

move. If it moves materially, the table should be corrected again.

## 9. Conclusion
Twenty years ago a small room of men around a pool table made a game about stealing cars, and critics and

players graded its sequels about fifteen points apart for two decades, close enough to still be arguing about

the same games. The pipeline that built that room was replaced: by consultancies selling representation as a

service, by owners publishing composition targets in annual reports, by a generation of makers who say, in

print, that the work comes out of their own mothers, their own identities, their own childhoods, and their

own politics. The products changed in ways that can be listed, counted, and watched. The critic layer that

should have reported the change instead curated its membership, graded the change at 99 and 82 and 62, and

watched the players answer 3.7 and 4.0 and 1.7. And when the players left, they were told the product was

never for them, that a modern audience was coming, that they were a loud minority, an incel culture, men

evolving too slowly.

The modern audience has had twenty years to arrive. The numbers in this paper are what arrived instead.

The franchises were not beaten by a rival. They were handed over, politely, in writing, by the people who

inherited them, to themselves. What is left is a pulp of the original vision, with the original name still on the

cover, selling on the credit of the men who built it and the audience that has not yet finished leaving.
