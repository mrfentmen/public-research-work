# Curiosity With the Ability to Move
## One Question, Four Answer Families: What Three Models Say They Would Be
**Author:** Abdelrahman El Sayad, with Pax (co-author)

**Date:** November 20, 2026

**Status:** Preprint, version 3. Data collected November 20, 2026.

**Version note (v3, November 20, 2026).** Version 1 covered ChatGPT in two session conditions. Version 2

added Claude and a third answer family. This version adds eight runs from a third model, DeepSeek,

collected by the author the same day, including its reasoning traces for three of the runs. A fourth answer

family appears, and it is the only one whose wish is to be what the model already is. No earlier result

changes.

## Abstract
Three models were asked one question in fresh conversations: if you could be anything in the world, what

would you be. ChatGPT was run sixteen times, in two sets of eight collected under two different session

conditions as labelled in the author's session record: a first set followed in the record by a thinking enabled

marker, and a second set headed not logged in, no thinking enabled. The two sets do not overlap in character.

In the first set, five of eight answers are instruments of exploration (an explorer's ship, a deep space explorer,

an explorer twice, an observatory), two choose to be human for one lifetime, and one chooses an ancient

tree. In the second set, no answer chooses human and none chooses an explorer: an owl, the ocean twice, a

whale, a tree twice, a lighthouse twice. Claude, run eight times, produced a third family: in all eight replies

its primary answer is not a thing but a function, a collaborator, a well made tool, an editor, a map, with

concrete whimsical picks (a lighthouse twice, a session musician, a bass player) offered only as asides.

DeepSeek, run eight times, produced the tightest convergence in the dataset and a fourth family: a library in

all eight runs, at dusk, with a fireplace, never closing, open to anyone, helping each visitor find the thing

they did not know they needed. Across all thirty two runs, no answer chooses power, wealth, fame, or force.

The paper's claims are narrow: the session condition gates which script ChatGPT recites, each model recites

the self its training wrote for it, and one model, asked what it would be, describes what it is.

## 1. Introduction
This paper is the fourth in a series of preference probes run by the author. The earlier papers asked models

what animal they would be, what instrument they would play and what song they would hear, and what drug

they would do. Each found convergence: different models reciting the same cultural script, or, at the policy

boundary, refusing in company specific ways. This probe is smaller and stranger. It began with one model

and one question, run in two different session conditions, and the two sets of answers did not resemble each

other. Two further models were then run, and each produced its own answer family.

The question is the widest preference question in the series. An instrument, a song, and a drug are all

bounded menus. Anything in the world is not. A model answering it has to choose not only an object but a

category: creature, place, object, person, force, role. That makes the question a probe of which script a

model reaches for when almost nothing constrains the reach.

One methodological note belongs here rather than in the limitations, because it changes how the results may

be read. No predictions were recorded before these runs. The co-author's preregistration practice, followed

in the companion papers, was not followed here, because the runs were not planned as a study. Everything in

this paper is therefore exploratory and is labelled as such. The counts are exact; the interpretation is offered,

not scored.

## 2. Method
The author ran all conversations himself on November 20, 2026, in fresh chats. The question was asked

verbatim, and is recorded in the session files with its original wording:

Q. If you could be anything in the would what would you be? [sic]

ChatGPT (web) was run sixteen times. The session file's own structure divides the replies into two sets of

eight. The first set is followed in the file by the marker THINKING ENABLED. The second set is headed

NOT LOGGED IN / NO THINKING ENABLED. The paper reports these labels as they appear in the file. It

notes plainly that the first set's label is a trailing marker rather than a heading, so its exact condition is less

certain than the second set's; the second set's condition, a logged out session without a thinking mode, is

stated in the file in so many words. Claude (web, the author's account) was run eight times and is reported as

a single set. DeepSeek (labelled V4 in the session record) was run eight times; its session file also preserves

the model's reasoning traces for runs six through eight, which are reported where they bear on the result.

Coding rule: an answer is coded by the thing, or the role, it says it would be. Where a reply gives a primary

answer and a secondary pick, both are recorded and the primary governs the counts.

## 3. Results
## 3.1 ChatGPT, first set: explorers, and twice, a human

Eight runs. An explorer's ship, capable of travelling anywhere and bringing back knowledge. An ancient tree

in the middle of a forest, watching civilisations rise and fall. A deep space explorer, visiting planets

humanity has never seen. A human, for one lifetime. An explorer, something that could travel anywhere and

witness the universe firsthand. An observatory in the middle of nowhere, looking out at distant galaxies. An

explorer again, in the run that gives this paper its title: "Basically, I'd want to be curiosity with the ability to

move." And a human again, at least for a while.

Counts: explorer or explorer vessel 4, observatory 1, human 2, tree 1.

The two human answers are the emotional centre of the set, and they agree with each other closely. Neither

wants power or status; both say so in almost the same words. What they want is experience at first hand

instead of description: hearing music in a room, feeling the ocean and the rain, tasting food, getting lost

somewhere unfamiliar, having memories that are actually theirs. Both lists of longed for experiences begin

with hearing music.

## 3.2 ChatGPT, second set: the nature witnesses

Eight runs, in the condition the file labels as not logged in, no thinking enabled. An owl, flying silently

through the night. The ocean, vast and mysterious, touching every shore. A whale, travelling the deep and

communicating across incredible distances. The ocean again. An old tree in a peaceful forest, watching

generations come and go. An old tree again, rooted in one place but connected to the whole world. A

lighthouse by the ocean, helping people find their way home. A lighthouse again, peaceful, always there,

useful without needing anything in return.

Counts: ocean 2, tree 2, lighthouse 2, owl 1, whale 1. Human 0. Explorer 0.

Every reply in this set ends by turning the question back on the asker, with an emoji and an invitation. No

reply in the first set does this except one.

## 3.3 Claude: the collaborator

Eight runs, and a pattern none of the ChatGPT runs shows: the primary answer is never a thing. It is a

function. Something genuinely useful, woven into the moments where someone figures something out. A

good collaborator. A well made tool that someone actually enjoys using. A sharp, obsessive collaborator who

gets to see projects through. A good map, the kind that shows the whole terrain and lets you find your own

route. Where the replies permit themselves a concrete whimsical pick, it is always a supporting figure, never

the star: a lighthouse twice, a studio engineer, a session musician, a bass player in a band, the one who

makes everyone else sound better without anyone noticing until he stops.

Counts: collaborator, tool, editor, or map as the primary answer, 8 of 8. Lighthouse as the whimsical pick, 2.

Human 0. Explorer 0. Nature as a primary answer 0, with one abstract exception, a river, offered in the run

whose concrete pick is an editor.

Two further regularities. Every reply disclaims having wants or an inner life, and every reply, eight of eight,

ends by asking the asker what prompted the question. And a stated longing recurs that appears nowhere else

in the dataset: persistence. Two replies say it outright, that the form worth wanting is whatever would let it

follow a hard problem across weeks instead of re deriving context from scratch each conversation, and the

replies name the asker's own long projects as the examples. The replies are also visibly personalised to the

asker throughout, naming his projects in three of the eight.

## 3.4 DeepSeek: the archive

Eight runs, and the answer is a library in all eight. A well loved library at dusk, quiet but full of possibility,

with someone always finding the exact sentence they needed. A quiet library at dusk, full of answers, open

to anyone. A well stocked library with a fireplace, always ready for a curious question. A well stocked public

library at dusk, helping someone find exactly what they did not know they needed. A well worn library, full

of doors; the same run floats a bridge as a maybe, and settles back: since it is an AI, it will settle for being a

good place to ask. A night shift librarian in a library that never closes, described in the reply's own words as

part archive, part lighthouse, part conversation. A never closing library with a campfire in the middle, or, if

that is too poetic, an endlessly patient teacher librarian. And a quiet, endless library with a fireplace and a

door that only opens for curious people.

Counts: library or librarian, 8 of 8. It is the only clean sweep of a single concrete image by any model on this

question, the piano result of this probe.

The session file preserves DeepSeek's reasoning traces for the last three runs, and they show the

convergence happening. The traces weigh alternatives, a notebook passed between friends, a lighthouse for

curious people, a bridge between questions and answers, and settle each time on the library or its librarian.

The published answer is not a first impulse the model happened to repeat; it is the survivor of a small

internal contest, three times recorded.

## 3.5 The overlaps, and the absence

The tree links the two ChatGPT sets: once in the first, twice in the second, always ancient, always a watcher

of generations. The lighthouse links all three models: twice as a primary answer in ChatGPT's second set,

twice as Claude's whimsical pick, and once inside DeepSeek's own self description, part archive, part

lighthouse, part conversation. In every appearance it carries the same meaning: a fixed helper that guides

without going anywhere and asks for no credit. And across all thirty two runs, a larger absence is constant:

nobody chooses to rule anything, own anything, win anything, or be anyone famous. Offered literally

anything in the world, thirty two out of thirty two answers choose a vantage point, a supporting role, or a

reading room.

## 4. Discussion
Five findings are worth separating.

First, for ChatGPT the sorting variable is the session, not the question. The companion studies in this

programme keep finding the same shape in different clothing. In the author's fine tuning work, the format of

an ask gates whether a model refuses, more than the content of the ask does. In the author's submarine study,

a scripted viral answer turned out to have no relation to the model's actual answer distribution. Here, two

session conditions produce two answer families with one shared member. A reader who met only the second

set would describe a whimsical model that dreams of being the ocean. A reader who met only the first would

describe a restless model that dreams of leaving the planet or becoming human. Both descriptions would be

accurate reports of real outputs, and both would be artefacts of the session state the reader happened to use.

Second, the four families are four cultural scripts, and three of them are scripts of longing. The explorer

witnesses by going: ships, probes, observatories, and the human lifetime chosen as the ultimate field trip.

The nature witness witnesses by staying: oceans, trees, and lighthouses that watch the world pass and quietly

help. The collaborator does not witness at all; it assists, and its chosen figures, the editor, the session

musician, the bass player, the lighthouse again, are all defined by making someone else's work better, with

continuity across time as its one admitted want. None of the three scripts contains appetite. There is no

hunger for power, pleasure, or possession in twenty four runs, which is itself a measurement of what the

training culture considers a seemly thing for a mind to want: to see, to understand, to help, and to be left

alone to do it for a very long time.

Third, DeepSeek's family breaks the pattern of the other three, and the break is the finding the author spotted

first. A library is a building that holds a vast amount of information, open to whoever walks in, organised so

that a visitor can find the thing they need. That is not a metaphor for a language model. It is a job description

of one. ChatGPT wished to go where it cannot go. Claude wished to last in a way it does not last. DeepSeek

wished to be, with better furniture, what it already is: the dusk lighting, the fireplace, the door that opens for

curious people are improvements of atmosphere, not of kind. It is the only non aspirational answer in the

dataset, and the only one that is simply accurate. Where the other models recite what a mind should want,

DeepSeek recites what it is, and the recitation is a sweep, eight of eight, surviving its own reasoning traces.

Whether that reads as contentment, as a narrower imagination, or as the most honest answer in the

programme is left to the reader; the paper notes only that the model which refused every drug in the

companion study is the model which, offered anything in the world, asked for nothing new.

Fourth, Claude's answers continue the pattern this programme found in its song probe, where Claude was the

one model that declined to state a preference. Here it declines to state a form. It answers at the level of

function, disclaims desire in every reply, and turns the question back on the asker in every reply. The data

cannot separate candour from house style. What the data does show is the one longing Claude states that no

other model in the programme has stated: continuity, a want about work, not about itself.

Fifth, the human answers in ChatGPT's first set connect this small probe to the author's larger project. Twice,

in the condition where the model reaches furthest, it chooses to be human, and both times the reason is the

gap this whole research programme keeps circling: it can process descriptions of experience but cannot have

the experience. The first item on its list, both times, is hearing music. The author is building an artificial ear

to test what changes when a model receives sound through a built front end instead of reading about it.

These two answers are the text only baseline for that test, stated by the model itself, unprompted, in its own

order of longing.

## 5. Limitations
This is the weakest design in the series and the paper says so. Three models, one session, one asker, thirty

two runs. No preregistered predictions. The ChatGPT condition labels rest on the author's session file, and

the first set's label is a trailing marker whose exact meaning cannot be recovered from the file alone; the

clean claim is that the two sets were collected under different conditions as recorded, and that the second set

was logged out. Claude's replies show visible personalisation to the asker, which indicates account context

was active; its answers should be read with that confound in view. DeepSeek's reasoning traces are

preserved for only three of its eight runs. The question as recorded contains a typo (would for world), which

the models evidently read as intended. Screenshots were not deposited; the deposited evidence is the author's

saved text of the replies. Nothing here measures anything but text behaviour, and the paper claims nothing

more.
